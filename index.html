The Beginning

From Artist to Engineer

I didn't start as an engineer. I studied graphic design at East Carolina University and spent my early career in print. But I quickly realized that static design wasn't enough — I wanted to build things that moved and did something.

At a catalog company, I taught myself AppleScript and automated a three-month production process down to two weeks. I literally coded myself out of a job — but it set my career in motion.

~1998-2000

QuarkXPress in the Browser

Before React, before modern web frameworks, I was experimenting with building QuarkXPress-like document layouts directly in the browser. This was the era of IE6 and table-based layouts — the idea of rich document editing in a browser was considered impossible.

The PoC never shipped, but it taught me to question conventional wisdom about what browsers could do. Ten years later, tools like Google Docs proved the concept at scale.

Problem Solving

The KNN Story

My proudest technical moment at Cavalier came when a third-party API for checking service availability proved too slow — killing our conversion funnel before customers could even sign up.

I implemented a K-Nearest Neighbor algorithm to predict service availability using geolocation and historical customer data. The result? Online conversions doubled overnight.

That experience taught me what I love most — solving real-world problems through smart automation and creative engineering. Sometimes the best solution isn't waiting for the API to respond — it's building your own intelligence layer.
